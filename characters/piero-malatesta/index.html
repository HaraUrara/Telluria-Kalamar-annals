Piero Malatesta

Player Character

Renaaria Bay is well-known for its merchant princes, individuals who have achieved sovereignty not because of an accident of birth, but through their own strength of will. The merchant prince exemplifies the belief that destiny is in one’s hand, and that power lies within the individual. No other city is this more evident than in Zoa, the City of Sails. Here, in its sprawling marketplace, bustling trading posts, and busy ports, many a family name have seen its rise – and fall – plying their trades with the hopes that someday their names too will give birth to its own merchant prince.

One of these trading families is the Malatesta line. Established by the patriarch, Cosimo, through shrewd business-sense and plain luck, the family’s wealth is grounded primarily in the trade of textiles (rugs, cloths, etc.). The family has prospered even further by attaching itself to one of the major families in Zoa. However, the pursuit of mercantile power is not a race with only one participant.

The Malatesta line, much like the majority of the trade families in Zoa, is embroiled in an embittered feud with another family – the Montefeltros. The true cause of the bad-blood between the two families is shrouded in the mists of legend. Some say that it was because of a business deal gone wrong; while some others believe that it was the fact that the first Malatesta and the first Montefeltro were once brothers, and that they were cursed to forever cause each other distress. Regardless, the enmity between the two families penetrates their daily lives – Malatesta will not suffer Montefeltro, and vice versa. This deep-seated hatred came to a head when the Malatesta scion, Piero, was involved in a brutal episode of the two families’ vendetta that would leave the young Malatesta exiled and disowned.

It began with the death of his twin, Paolo. Challenged to a duel before the eyes of his peers and the public, Paolo was more than happy to humiliate the hated Montefeltro. The fact that that particular Montefeltro was the brothers’ own bitter rival, Bernardo, was simply the sweet cream atop the pastry. To Piero’s sorrow, however, the gods did not look favorably upon the twin. Felled by a blow from Bernardo’s sword, Paolo was defeated. No sooner did Pietro’s body lay cold, Piero sought Bernardo and challenged him to a duel. The answer astounded Piero – “No,” Bernardo replied. Enraged and bitter, Piero could barely contain his anger, and it took all of his resolve not to strike down the blasted Montefeltro. But Piero was in the wrong, and he knew. Duels are sanctioned, with consent from both parties. Seething with impotent rage, Piero stormed off, suffering the jeers and laughter as he went.

That evening, Piero drank, nursing his grief at the bottom of a bottle. As the night wore on, accompanied by his lackeys, he searched for trouble. They did not search for long. Through the haze of inebriation, Piero was convinced that he saw Bernardo walking down the street. A bloodlust suddenly consumed the Malatesta, and called for his men to hound the suspected Montefeltro. The fact that that familiar man ran from the mob convinced them even more of his identity. They pursued him through alleyways and street-corners. Fast as the man was, eventually the chase ended – and there the beating began. Mercilessly, the drunken mob came down on the man, raining blows upon blows on him. It was the intervention of a city guard that prevented the assault from turning into a murder.

Come morning, the identity of the man soon came to light. Piero was right to suspect that the man was a Montefeltro, for the victim was one. However, it was the wrong member. It was a second cousin; a member of a minor branch, yet one that had influence. Regardless, as news of the beating spread quickly, reprisal soon came. Malatesta family members, minor as they were, were attacked. A nephew was beaten by a mob, a trade-client was intimidated, and supplies were sabotaged. The Malatestas, on their end, sought a counter-reprisal. Weeks went by as Malatesta and Montefeltro fell into a cycle of revenge – one attacked, then one counter-attacked. The climax of this public vendetta occurred when Piero was involved in a night attack upon a Montefeltro dyeing facility.

He heard from his spies that Bernardo would be at that dyeing facility located at the docks. That night, Piero resolved to take satisfaction from his brother’s killer. Along with his most trusted circle, and their lackeys, Piero launched an ambush on the dyeing facility, laying a siege on the building. As fighting between Malatesta men and Montefeltro goons grew more and more intense, a boiler exploded, destroying the better part of a public port. In the confusion, Bernardo was able to slip past the melee and escape.

There are many crimes in Zoa, but none is more treasonous than the disruption of its life-blood – trade. The boiler explosion left that port inoperable for months, and therefore the supplies and goods that the businesses around the area depended upon. Official Zoan intervention was deemed necessary then and there. As the most prominent member of the Malatesta line involved, the blame fell on Piero. An execution was whispered among the public, but exile would be Piero’s destiny. Ultimately labelled as a disappointment of the family, the eldest son of the Malatesta line would be cast out, unwelcome in Zoa. Piero was outraged. He did nothing wrong, the thought. He defended the family honor, he defended the family name – the very same family that would rather see him gone. Worse still, his brother’s killer, Bernardo Montefeltro, is yet free.

Bloodied, bruised, but not beaten, Piero gathered his belongings, and was determined to do one thing – blaze his own trail, and take hold of his own destiny. He would seek strength outside of the Zoa, outside of his family’s influence. Piero will return one day. He remembered his mother saying something about an uncle living in a faraway place. What was the name of that place again? Piero thought to himself.

Parol? Piss-all? Pekal…?
